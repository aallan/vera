- The negation of an `@Int` is obligated and guarded like `0 - x` ([#1875](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1875)).  `-@Int.0` at `INT_MIN` returned `INT_MIN`, and a contract `vera verify` proved over a negation failed at run time at that one input.  Each negation of an `@Int` that is not a literal now carries an `int_overflow` obligation (E528), in a body, a `requires`, an `ensures`, a refinement predicate, a `decreases` measure, an `assert`, a closure and a `where` function, and traps with `Integer overflow` at `INT_MIN` where it is not proved.  A function that negates a parameter either excludes `INT_MIN` with `requires(@Int.0 > -9223372036854775808)`, which proves the obligation, or keeps a run-time check (Tier 3).  A negated literal such as `-9223372036854775808` and the negation of a `@Nat` carry none.  `examples/absolute_value.vera` and every absolute value the documents teach gain that precondition.
