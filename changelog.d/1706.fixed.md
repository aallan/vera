- **A call's facts are assumed only where its arguments fit the callee's parameters** ([#1706](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1706)).  The verifier modelled a call to a user function by assuming the callee's `ensures`, and its refined return's predicate, about a fresh result under the path's conditions alone, and it discharged the obligation each argument owes its parameter after the body was translated, with those facts among its premises.  So `nat_to_int(nat_id(@Int.0))`, with `nat_id(@Nat -> @Nat)` ensuring `@Nat.result == @Nat.0`, recorded the argument's narrowing into `@Nat` verified at Tier 1 from `nat_id`'s own `ret == @Int.0` and `ret >= 0`, and `vera run` trapped on `-1` (`Negative value bound into a @Nat slot`).  The same facts discharged a refined parameter's predicate, a refinement written inside the formal (an `Option<PosInt>` payload, through an `ensures` that names it or through a `match` on the parameter, whose binder facts were assumed of the argument), a constructor field inside the argument, a `let` narrowing of the value the call then receives, and a `let` whose `@Nat` slot is itself the argument.  The callee's facts are now assumed only under what its own verification assumed of its parameters, stated over the actual arguments: `>= 0` for a `@Nat`, a refinement's predicate, and the refinements written inside a parameter's type, for every argument whether or not it narrows at the call, and the facts a `match` in the callee's contract assumes about its parameter are under the same condition.  A call's result now carries the refinements written inside its return type beside its head refinement, through the disclosure gate, since a result was the one producer of such a value the verifier held no fact for: `consume(mk(x))`, with `mk` returning `Option<PosInt>`, keeps `consume`'s postcondition where `mk` proves its payload.  A program whose call-argument narrowing was recorded verified only through the callee's postcondition now fails `vera verify` with `E503` (`E505` for a refined parameter) at the call, as `let @Nat = @Int.0` already does, and the fix is a `requires` on the caller or an enclosing branch; the runtime behaviour of such programs is unchanged (they trapped before and trap now).  A proof after the call that uses the callee's postcondition keeps it wherever the arguments are shown to fit.  Spec §6.4.2 states the rule.  `tests/test_call_facts_after_obligations_1706.py` holds the class: 400 cells over the formal (`@Nat`, a refinement of `@Int`, a refinement of `@Nat`, a refinement inside `Option<PosInt>`), the callee's facts (an `ensures` equating result and parameter, one bounding the parameter below by the result, one bounding it above, a refined return of another type related to the parameter, and `ensures(true)`), the argument (the bare parameter, an expression over it, the parameter under a proving `requires`, under a proving `if`) and the call's position (the return expression, a `let` value, an outer call's argument, an `if` condition, a `match` arm), 115 of them red at 313c04db; and 24 tests for the issue's program, the shapes beside it, what the guard must not move and one killer per part of the guard, 16 of them red at 313c04db.  The `examples/` programs, the `tests/conformance/` programs, the probes and the documentation blocks verify exactly as before.
