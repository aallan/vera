- **The collector no longer marks a block on the free list, so a word that names a freed block, a stale word in the slack of a block `$alloc` reused whole or an integer equal to its address, no longer keeps the free chain behind it and every stale graph those blocks still name** ([#1718](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1718)).  The sweep links a freed block through its first payload word and leaves the rest, `$alloc` reuses a block whole, and Phase 1 recorded every block on the allocation chain as an object base, so one such word marked a free block, its free-list link marked the next, and memory the program no longer held was retained at every collection that met a free list.  Under `VERA_EAGER_GC=1`, 58 of the 146 runnable corpus programs that carry the collector marked free blocks, `examples/gc_pressure.vera` 43,500 over 29 collections, up to its whole free list of 2,900 blocks in one; the ordinary collector marked them in a `Map<String, Int>` of 100 keys and in an `Array<String>`, a `Map<String, Int>` and a list of 20,000 values each.  Phase 1b now clears the object-base bit of every block on the free list, so `$gc_is_base` refuses a free block at the shadow-stack seed, the drain and the rescan alike.  It costs one step per free block per collection and nothing per allocation, and `examples/gc_pressure.vera` runs about 12 times faster under `VERA_EAGER_GC=1`, the free chain it kept no longer marked and scanned at each collection.  `VERA_GC_CHECK_MARKS=1` also traps on a mark store to a block on the free list, which it finds by walking the list.
