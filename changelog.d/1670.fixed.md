- **A stress test runs under a budget of 600 s, and a test can name a budget of its own** ([#1670](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1670)).  `tests/pipeline.py` ends the process when a stage call is still running at the end of its budget, 120 s for a call that names none, and the `stress`-marked tests named none.  The 10,000-insert `map_insert` chain of `tests/test_codegen_gc_reclamation.py::TestHostHandleReclamation573::test_map_chain_reclaims_transients`, which took from 48.1 s to 94.3 s of call time in three runs on a 12-core Mac, ran past it on a GitHub runner five times in the runs of [#1669](https://github.com/aallan/vera/pull/1669) and [#1672](https://github.com/aallan/vera/pull/1672), three times in a row at one head, and each time ended the `stress` job before the stress tests after it ran.  The new `budget(seconds)` marker runs a test under a budget of its own, and a `stress`-marked test that names none runs under 600 s: `tests/conftest.py` holds the whole test to it, so each stage call in the test that names no `budget_s=` runs under the test's budget, a call that names its own keeps it, and a test with neither marker runs as before.  `tests/test_budget_marker_1670.py` holds this in eight cells, under pytest-xdist and without it, with the default lowered to 0.5 s and a stage call that takes 1.5 s: unmarked, the call is ended and the run names the test; marked `budget(5)` or `stress`, the test passes; and inside a `budget(5)` test, a call that passes `budget_s=0.5` is ended at its own budget.  The stress workflow now also runs on a pull request that changes `tests/conftest.py` or `tests/pipeline.py`, the two files that decide the budget a stress test runs under.
