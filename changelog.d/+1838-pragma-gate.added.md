- **A mutation pragma is filed under the line it is written on** ([#1838](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1838)).  mutmut files a `# pragma: no mutate` by its place in the syntax tree, so one written after the closing bracket of a statement that spans lines protected the statement's first line instead of its own.  `scripts/check_pragma_placement.py` reads each pragma with `tokenize` and without the engine, and accepts it only in a shape it places by a local rule, filed under its own line: after the code of a line that starts its statement, outside every bracket; a bare pragma after code inside brackets; a `block`, `start` or `end` alone directly above a statement at its column; an `end` alone directly below a statement at its column, ending its block or the module.  Every other shape is refused with a message saying where to write the pragma, including some that mutmut reads, since where libcst puts those turns on more than their neighbouring lines (a block's or a module's footer, a clause's own leading lines, a comment line after a backslash).  It runs in the commit hook and CI's `lint` job, and its tests, a generated matrix of every place a comment can go among them, hold every pragma it accepts to mutmut's own visitor in the Mutation (diff) lane, and hold each documented shape accepted.  The nine pragmas it found under `vera/` are moved onto the lines they answer or deleted, and the mutants the old places suppressed by accident are answered.  Three of the conditions they sat on (two in `_closure_argument_component`, one in `_emit_written_store_guard`) also carried four correctly filed pragmas whose reason was "unreachable", which hid the mutants that make the condition true, and the tests kill those: the three conditions are split into one test per operand, and no pragma is left on them.
