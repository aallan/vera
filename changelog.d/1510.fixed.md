- **The collector marks a heap of any width: a full mark worklist no longer traps** ([#1510](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1510)).  `$gc_collect` marked through a worklist of 16,384 entries, and a push that found it full executed `unreachable`, so a correct program died in the collector whenever a collection ran with more values pending at once than that, in a heap it had already allocated.  The issue's 20,000-element `Array<String>` trapped inside `array_map`, and under ordinary collection so did an `Array<String>` of 16,385 strings, a list of a user `data` 16,384 cells long, an array of 16,385 boxed closures each capturing a `String`, and a string-keyed `Map` built by `map_insert` from 596 keys, natively, in the browser runtime and under `--target wasi-p2`.  A child push that finds the worklist full is now dropped and the object being scanned recorded as dirty: the lowest dirty object with the word its push was dropped at, and the highest.  Once the worklist empties, that range is walked along the allocation chain and each marked object in it scanned again, until a drain drops nothing.  A walk that fills the worklist stops there, and the object and word it stopped at become the resume point: a later walk resumes that object at that word.  A drop recorded below it lowers the range's bottom but not the resume point, and a walk jumps from the highest such drop straight to the object it resumes, so while the drops below the resume object lie together, a collection's walks visit each word and header of their range at most once.  Against a worklist that drops nothing, the rescan adds 1.56% to the collector's instructions for an `Array<String>` of 100,000 strings, whose elements push nothing of their own.  For records of two `String` values each, it adds 46% and 55% at 50,000 and 200,000 records in an array built by `array_map` (34% and 36% of the whole program), and 47%, 56% and 55% at 150,000, 600,000 and 1,200,000 records joined by a three-way `array_concat` into an array that lies above them (34%, 28% and 19%).  The worklist bounds the collector's memory, not the heap it marks, and the memory layout is unchanged.  The seed's push loses its capacity test, since it pushes at most one entry per shadow-stack root and the 4,096 roots always fit, and code generation refuses a layout whose worklist is smaller than its shadow stack.  The `unreachable` trap's Fix paragraph names four causes; the mark worklist is no longer one.
