- **A verifier fix offers a `requires` only in the function entry's terms** ([#1858](https://github.com/aallan/vera/issues/1858)).  `E526`, `E527`, `E505` and `E501` wrote a precondition from the operands as the site has them, and a slot reference at the entry names the nearest binding of its type there: for `let @Int = @Int.0 - 1; 10 / @Int.0`, `E526` offered `` `requires(@Int.0 != 0)` ``, a fact about the parameter, and with it in place `E526` fired again.  `E526`, `E505` and `E501` now offer a clause only where it reads at the entry as at the site and the solver confirms it discharges the obligation, the rule `E502` and `E503` follow; otherwise the sentence stands without the example.  `E527` offers none, since it fires only where the premises prove the index out of bounds, so no clause added beside them can discharge it.  `E505`'s clause is the refinement's predicate at the narrowed value (`` `requires(@Int.0 - 1 > 0)` `` for `let @Pos = @Int.0 - 1`), where it pasted the predicate over its binder, `` `requires(@Int.0 > 0)` ``, which did not clear it even for a parameter.  The guard each of `E526`, `E527` and `E501` names is written around the operation, with braces and a `<default>` placeholder, so it parses as Vera, and by the canonical formatter, so `10 / (@Int.0 - @Int.1)` keeps its parentheses; `E501`'s guard and clause are backticked spans.  A call to a `where` helper whose precondition may not hold is reported as any other call is, with its "At this call site" line, its guard and its clause, where it had the general sentence.
